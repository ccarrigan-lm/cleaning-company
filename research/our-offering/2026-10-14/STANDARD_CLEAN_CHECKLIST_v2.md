# Our standard clean – checklist, revision 2 (15 Oct 2026)

**Status: DRAFT for discussion. Supersedes the 14 Oct draft in this folder, which is kept unchanged for comparison.**
- **No prices are set.** Every price is provisional until pilot homes give us real cleaner-minutes and we have loaded labour cost.
- **Not yet tested in a real home.** Tasks marked ⏱ are timed in the pilot homes before they are priced.
- Items marked **PROPOSED** are starting positions awaiting the owner's approval (see "Decisions" at the end).

**Built from** the Louisville checklist evidence in `research/checklists/2026-10-14/` (task sources, URLs, capture dates and quoted text are in `task_matrix_long.csv` and `raw/`). "5/5" means all five instant-price competitors include the task in their standard clean: Scrub Lou, Maids in Apron, Super Cleaning, Red Feather and Maid 2 Clean.

**How to read each task.**
- **Cleaner does:** the specific action and the "done" standard.
- **Customer sees:** the wording for our public checklist.
- **Limit:** what is outside the task, and what happens then.

## Ground rules for every standard clean

1. **Reach. (PROPOSED)** We clean what can be reached standing on the floor, plus extension tools (extension duster, extension pole). Nothing is climbed.
   - Whether to add a specific step stool is decided after reviewing equipment and working practice with our cleaners.
   - Market norm for reference: R&R allows a 2-step ladder, Scrub Lou a 3-step; Super Cleaning brings no ladders; Maids in Apron allows no stools.
2. **Furniture.** We don't move beds, sofas, dressers, appliances or anything heavy. We clean around them, and under them where our tools reach.
3. **Items on surfaces.** We move a manageable number of ordinary small items (decor, toiletries, remotes, countertop items), clean underneath and put them back where they were. We don't pick up, sort or put away belongings, dishes or laundry. Where a surface is genuinely blocked, the cleaner records it as **not completed – blocked by items** and it appears on the customer's visit record. It is never skipped silently.
4. **Price and scope.** The price is fixed in advance from the rooms selected, the scope and the condition the customer disclosed (with photos where we ask for them).
   - If the clean takes longer than **we** estimated, the agreed price stands. That is our estimating risk, and the time is logged so we can correct future estimates.
   - If the home's actual condition or the requested scope differs **materially** from what was disclosed, the cleaner pauses before doing the additional work and contacts the office. The customer chooses either a **revised fixed price** for the full scope or a **reduced scope** at the agreed price. Nothing extra is done or charged without that agreement.
   - "Material difference" is judged against written condition examples with photos (normal maintenance vs substantial buildup), not against the cleaner's speed.
5. **First visit. (PROPOSED)** No automatic deep-clean requirement. The first visit is priced from the condition and scope we assess (photos or walkthrough). Time since the last professional clean is not enough on its own to require a deep clean.
6. **Equipment and tool hygiene. (PROPOSED)** We bring all supplies and equipment, including vacuum and mop.
   - Separate cloths for toilets/bathrooms and for kitchens, kept apart by colour, and never used on the other area.
   - Fresh cloths and fresh mop pads for every home; used ones are bagged and laundered, not reused that day.
   - Vacuum, mop and caddy cleaned between visits, recorded on the equipment log.
7. **Product wording.** Customer-facing text says "cleaned". We only say "disinfected" or "sanitized" for a task whose cleaner instructions name the product, the surfaces it is compatible with, and its label-required application and contact time, and the cleaner follows them.
8. **Never in scope:** biohazards (pet waste, bodily fluids, mold, pests); hoarding conditions; moving heavy furniture; climbing ladders; hauling trash beyond the household bins; exterior windows; wet-wiping light bulbs; restoring damaged surfaces.
9. **Completion record and guarantee.** Before leaving, the cleaner marks every task in every room as:
   - **Completed**
   - **Not applicable** (the room doesn't have it, e.g. no ceiling fan)
   - **Not completed**, with a reason (blocked by items, beyond reach, fixture unsafe, customer asked to skip, condition beyond scope, out of time)

   The customer receives the record after the visit, listing anything not completed and why. They may report anything missed within 24 hours for a free return visit (market norm: Scrub Lou, R&R, MaidPro, Maids in Apron).

## Pricing structure notes for the room-based model

- **Basements.** A basement is priced as the rooms or areas the customer selects (e.g. a finished basement family room is a common room, a basement bath is a bath) at their normal scope. There is no blanket basement fee on top of the same rooms.
- **Pets.** Ordinary pet hair on floors is covered by W7/W8. Substantial hair buildup, or pet-hair removal from upholstery, is assessed separately (upholstery is an add-on below). No automatic charge for owning a pet.
- **Floors.** Floor time is estimated once per home from square footage and floor type, not again inside each room.

## Decisions (starting positions, awaiting approval)

Settle first, because it fixes the standard we test with the cleaners:

1. **Kitchen and bathroom promise.** Base service includes the microwave interior (K5), ordinary stovetop grease and spills (K3) and routine shower soap scum (B2). Substantial buildup is handled under rule 4.

Then:

2. **Reach:** floor plus extension tools now; a step stool only after reviewing equipment and practice with the cleaners.
3. **Linens:** bed made with existing bedding; sheet changes a per-bed extra selected at booking.
4. **First visit:** no automatic deep clean; price from assessed condition and scope.
5. **Tool hygiene:** rule 6 as written (separate cloths by area, fresh cloths and pads per home, equipment cleaning logged).
6. **Condition examples:** write the photo examples that define normal maintenance vs substantial buildup for stovetops, showers, floors and pet hair, so rule 4 is applied consistently.

## Pilot homes and timing

1. Start with **three varied pilot homes** (different sizes, bath counts, floor types and conditions).
2. Record, per visit:
   - actual **cleaner-minutes by room** (each bedroom, full bath, half bath, kitchen, common room) and for floors per 500 sq ft by floor type
   - **setup and packing** minutes, and **travel** minutes
   - the number of cleaners. Labour is counted in cleaner-hours: two cleaners working one hour is two labour-hours.
   - the home's condition against the condition examples, and every "not completed" item with its reason
   - ordinary vs heavily soiled times for K3, K5 and B2
3. Compare actual with estimated minutes; add trials only where the estimates stay uncertain.
4. Pair the minutes with loaded cost per cleaner-hour, supplies per visit and travel, and only then convert this checklist into room and service prices (see `research/analysis/2026-10-14/FINDINGS.md`, "Operational inputs needed").
